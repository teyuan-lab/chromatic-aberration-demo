How the model works & sources

Each ray is intersected analytically with two spherical surfaces, then refracted using vector Snell’s law at each surface. This is a symmetric biconvex lens in air (n = 1), with R₁ = +R, R₂ = −R and fixed center thickness 6 mm. Wavelength-dependent indices use SCHOTT’s three-term Sellmeier equation: n² − 1 = Σ Bᵢλ²/(λ² − Cᵢ), with λ in µm. Dimensions are in mm.

Back focal distances are axis intercepts of a ray entering 0.001 mm off axis, measured from the rear vertex. The rays and screen spots also include spherical aberration. Turning dispersion off assigns the 546.1 nm index to every color. Spot radii are measured about the optical axis using 700 uniform-area pupil samples per wavelength. Colors are illustrative; this is not a diffraction PSF or a simulated camera image. Fresnel losses, diffraction, absorption, and manufacturing errors are omitted.

Glass data: SCHOTT optical glass datasheets (N-BK7 and N-F2). Source code & validation.
