Natural minerals are represented by pure optical material models; fluorite and diamond measurements use synthetic specimens. All crystal choices are cubic and use scalar refraction. Crystalline quartz and calcite would require birefringent ray tracing.

How the model works & sources

Each ray is intersected analytically with two spherical surfaces, then refracted using vector Snell’s law at each surface. This is a symmetric biconvex lens in air (n = 1), with R₁ = +R, R₂ = −R and fixed center thickness 6 mm. Wavelength-dependent indices use published Sellmeier equations with one to four terms: n² − 1 = Σ Bᵢλ²/(λ² − Cᵢ), with λ in µm. Cubic ZnS uses n² = 8.393 + 0.14383/(λ² − 0.2421²) + 4430.99/(λ² − 36.71²). Dimensions are in mm. Sources and model notes appear with the selected material and in the comparison table. The simulator retains the n = 1 ambient idealization across datasets whose original index reference conditions differ slightly.

Back focal distances are axis intercepts of a ray entering 0.001 mm off axis, measured from the rear vertex. The rays and screen spots also include spherical aberration. Turning dispersion off assigns the 546.1 nm index to every color. Spot radii are measured about the optical axis using 700 uniform-area pupil samples per wavelength. Colors are illustrative; this is not a diffraction PSF or a simulated camera image. Rays undergoing total internal reflection (TIR) are marked at the rear surface and excluded from the first-pass screen spot; later internal bounces are not traced. Fresnel losses, diffraction, absorption, and manufacturing errors are omitted.
